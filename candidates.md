# Candidate Principles

This is the list of conduct principles waiting to be tested, with a proposed **floor** for each: the least that every tradition might plausibly ask. A proposed floor is a starting point for testing, not a finding. When a candidate is tested, its floor may be lowered further (method step 2) before any verdict.

The first fifteen candidates (C01 to C15) are the seed list from the project brief. C16 to C28 are further principles that recur across many of the traditions, found during the first research session (October 2026).

## Status key

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Untested | Not yet taken through the method |
| Testing | Currently being taken through the method |
| Admitted | Became an article (link given) |
| Register | Failed at every workable floor; became a Register entry (link given) |
| Rejected | Not a conduct principle, or out of scope |

## Flags

- **Split:** the seed bundled more than one duty, and the duties are likely to fare differently. Each part is tested separately.
- **Merged:** a principle found in research is folded into another candidate because it is a case of it.
- **Belief risk:** part of the principle is about belief rather than conduct (Section 2, rule 1). Only the conduct part is tested.
- **Likely contested:** early research suggests at least one tradition's texts may oppose the principle at its usual strength, so a lower floor or a Register entry is likely.

## The list

| ID | Candidate | Proposed floor | Where traditions may differ | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Treat others as you would want to be treated (the Golden Rule) | Do not treat other people in a way you would not want to be treated yourself. | Positive or negative form; whether the rule covers everyone or only one's own community; how it fits with punishment | **Register** ([Q-001](register/q-001-golden-rule.md)). Lower floor drafted as [Article I](code/articles/article-01-golden-rule.md) (version 0.3), held: Zoroastrianism (decision D1) and the wording on households and slavery (decision D2, evidence in the [annex](code/articles/article-01-annex-households.md)) |
| C02 | Do not kill the innocent | Never deliberately kill a person who has done no wrong. | Who counts as innocent; war, punishment and self-defence; commanded destruction in ancient war texts; unborn life and end of life | Untested. *Likely contested* at higher floors |
| C03 | Do not steal | Do not take what belongs to someone else without their consent. | Taking in dire need; spoils of war; some traditions forbid taking anything "not given", even trifles | Untested |
| C04 | Speak the truth; do not deceive | Do not lie to people or deceive them in order to harm them or take advantage of them. | Untruths to save a life or keep the peace are allowed in several traditions; Jainism's vow of truth is near-absolute | Untested. "Always tell the truth" likely needs a Register entry |
| C05 | Keep your promises | Keep the promises and agreements you make, and give back what was lent or entrusted to you, unless keeping a promise would mean doing wrong. | Release from vows; promises that conflict with what is right; whether to swear oaths at all | Untested. *Merged:* returning trusts and repaying debts |
| C06a | Protect the vulnerable | Do not take advantage of people who are weak, poor, sick or alone, and help them when you can. | How far the duty to help goes; who comes first | Untested. *Split* from seed 6. *Merged:* care for the sick |
| C06b | Protect the stranger | Do not mistreat people because they are foreigners or outsiders. | Who counts as a stranger; rules that treat insiders and outsiders differently | Untested. *Split* from seed 6 |
| C06c | Protect the guest | Do no harm to people who come to you in peace, and treat them with courtesy. | Hospitality is a strong duty in some traditions and barely mentioned in others | Untested. *Split* from seed 6. Weakest of the three |
| C07 | Honour and care for your parents and elders | Treat your parents with respect, and help them when they need you. | Obeying parents who ask for wrong; leaving home for a religious life; abusive parents; "elders" is much less widely taught than "parents" | Untested |
| C08 | Be generous to those in need | When you have more than you need, share some of it with people in need. | Fixed amounts (tithes, set shares) versus free giving; kin first or all alike | Untested |
| C09a | Master your anger | Do not act on anger in ways that harm other people. | Some traditions allow a rightful anger; others treat all anger as a fault | Untested. *Split* from seed 9. *Merged:* patience |
| C09b | Master your ego (humility) | Do not treat yourself as more important than other people, or boast in ways that belittle them. | Self-respect and proper dignity; "ego" itself is a belief term in several traditions | Untested. *Split* from seed 9. *Belief risk:* only the conduct is tested |
| C10 | Forgive | Be willing to forgive people who sincerely try to put right a wrong they did you. | Whether forgiveness is conditional on repentance; whether fair retaliation is allowed | Untested. To be tested together with C19 |
| C11a | Be fair in your dealings | Deal honestly with everyone: do not cheat, use false weights or measures, or hold back what you owe, including wages. | Rules that treat insiders and outsiders differently in trade or lending | Untested. *Split* from seed 11. *Merged:* honest weights; fair and prompt wages |
| C11b | Be just in judgement | Do not offer or accept bribes, and do not bend the truth or the rules to favour anyone when you judge, decide or give evidence. | Little real disagreement expected; the evidence may be thin in some traditions | Untested. *Split* from seed 11 |
| C12 | Be grateful | Recognise the good that others do for you, and return it when you can. | Gratitude to God is a matter of belief and is out of scope | Untested. *Belief risk:* only gratitude between people is tested |
| C13 | Practise moderation | Do not let drink, drugs or any excess lead you to harm others or yourself. | Total abstinence versus moderate use; whether harm to oneself is anyone else's business | Untested (floor taken from the brief) |
| C14a | Do not waste | Do not waste food, water or other things people need to live. | Little disagreement expected | Untested. *Split* from seed 14 |
| C14b | Care for the earth | Do not needlessly spoil or destroy the natural world that others, now and later, depend on. | "Dominion", "stewardship" and "sacredness" framings; the last is a belief | Untested. *Split* from seed 14. *Belief risk.* Weakest seed |
| C15 | Do not cause unnecessary suffering to living beings | Do not make any living creature suffer without need. | What counts as need: eating meat, sacrifice, medicine; some ancient philosophers held that animals are owed nothing | Untested |
| C16 | Do not slander or wound with words | Do not spread lies or malicious gossip about people, and do not use words to hurt them for no good reason. | Some traditions also forbid damaging talk that is *true*; others protect free criticism | Untested |
| C17 | Be faithful to your partner | Do not betray a partner by breaking the faithfulness you have promised them. | Whether all sex outside marriage is wrong; polygamy; divorce | Untested. *Likely contested* at any higher floor |
| C18 | Earn your living honestly | Get what you live on by means that do not cheat, exploit or harm others. | Some traditions honour living on alms; "everyone must work" would fail | Untested |
| C19 | Do not take revenge | Do not take revenge into your own hands; if you are wronged, seek redress only by fair means. | Fair and proportionate retaliation is allowed in several traditions; one tradition explicitly rejects "repay injury with kindness" | Untested. To be tested together with C10 |
| C20 | Look at your own faults first | Look honestly at your own faults before you condemn other people for theirs. | Several traditions *command* correcting others, and judges must judge, so the floor must not forbid judging | Untested |
| C21 | Make peace; do not stir up division | Do not stir up hatred or division between people, and help those in conflict to make peace when you can. | The positive half (making peace) is less widely taught than the negative half | Untested. May need splitting |
| C22 | Do not mock or humiliate | Do not mock or humiliate anyone, especially people who are poor, disabled or powerless. | Satire and fair criticism; texts built on social rank | Untested |
| C23 | Care for children | Care for children who depend on you: keep them safe, provide for them and help them grow up able to live well. | Discipline; many texts assume this duty rather than state it | Untested |
| C24 | Do not exploit need | Do not use another person's debt or desperate need to take advantage of them. | Whether charging interest is itself wrong (a likely Register question) | Untested. *Likely contested* at any higher floor |
| C25 | Do not let envy lead to wrong | Do not let envy of what others have lead you to take it or spoil it. | Coveting is a state of mind; only actions are in scope | Untested. *Belief risk:* may collapse into C03 and be rejected |
| C26 | Help someone in danger | If someone is in danger and you can help them without serious risk to yourself, help them. | How much risk one must accept | Untested |
| C27 | Do not force belief | Do not use force or threats to make anyone change their beliefs. | Historical laws on leaving a religion; laws protecting one faith | Untested. *Likely contested.* Kept because it recurs widely and is central to the Code's own stance |
| C28 | Use power for others' good | If you hold power over other people, use it for their good and not to exploit them. | How power should be held is a separate question; this floor concerns only how it is used | Untested |

**Total: 28 candidates** (15 seed principles and 13 further principles). Four seeds are split into parts, so there are 33 items to test in all.

## Considered and not listed separately

| Principle | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Honest weights and measures | Merged into C11a | It is the clearest case of fair dealing |
| Pay workers fairly and on time | Merged into C11a | A case of fair dealing; on its own it is thinly attested in several traditions |
| Return what is entrusted; repay debts | Merged into C05 | A trust or a loan is an implied promise |
| Care for the sick | Merged into C06a | A case of helping the vulnerable |
| Patience | Merged into C09a | The conduct side of patience is not acting on anger |
| Keeping confidences | Not listed | Can be tested as part of C04 or C05; thinly attested on its own |
| Seek knowledge and wisdom | Not listed | Mainly self-regarding and close to belief; not a rule about how to treat others |
