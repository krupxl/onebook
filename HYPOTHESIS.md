*None confirmed.* Two candidates have been isolated in Q-001 and are not yet tested:

1. **Grading treatment by allegiance** (Part 8). Whether courtesy, honour and company may rightly differ according to a person's faith or allegiance. Several traditions' texts grade such treatment; secular humanism rejects all prejudice.

## Current reading of the evidence

**C01, the Golden Rule.** No tradition's texts teach the opposite of the rule. But the first test, and especially the independent check, showed that agreement is narrower and less tidy than it first looked.

- **The harm floor** ("do not harm anyone in a way you would not want to be harmed yourself"): under a written counting rule, twelve traditions meet it in texts that count. Nine teach the test itself (putting yourself in the other's place); Sikhism and secular humanism meet it through a wider duty not to harm anyone; Taoism is borderline. Zoroastrianism states it explicitly, but only in Tier 2 texts that one major branch does not accept.
- **The full form** ("do not treat anyone in *any* way you would not want"): eight traditions clearly teach it. Taoism is borderline, the Digambara branch of Jainism is open, and Sikhism and secular humanism do not show the test itself in their primary texts.

Three things stand between the harm floor and the Code, and they are of different kinds:

1. **Procedure** (decision D1): how to count a principle stated only in Tier 2 texts that one branch accepts. This is about the project's rules, not about values.
2. **Institutions the ancient texts accepted** (decision D2): several primary texts accept slavery and physical discipline within households while limiting how people in them may be treated. Read today, the floor would condemn those institutions. This is the first place where a real difference may lie between what the texts permitted and what their own rule implies. It may turn out to be reducible (for example, if each tradition's own principles are shown to imply the end of these institutions, which would be a Logical resolution), or it may not.
3. **What counts as teaching the floor** (decision D3): whether a wider duty not to harm anyone counts. This is a question of method.

**What this means for the hypothesis so far.** One candidate is too few to judge. The result is still consistent with the hypothesis: no irreducible difference has been confirmed. But it is less comfortable than the first draft suggested. Two possibly axiomatic questions have been isolated, and the institutions question (D2) may prove harder to reduce than it looks. A pattern to watch: traditions whose primary texts are short (secular humanism) or survive mainly as liturgy (the Avesta) may fail on silence rather than disagreement, so the project may need a settled rule for silence, decided in advance.
