# Changelog

All changes to the Code, the Register and the method are recorded here, newest first. Dates are in ISO format (YYYY-MM-DD).

## 2026-10-13

### Households and slavery (decision D2): annex, second independent check, Article I version 0.4
- **New file `code/articles/article-01-annex-households.md`** (version 0.2): the evidence for decision D2 in all thirteen traditions, testing three wordings (no cruelty, no one owned, no hitting). One researcher and one independent verifier per tradition; 191 evidence rows, each with tier, translation, bearing and verification status.
- **Second independent check** (method step 7) of Article I version 0.3 and the annex by five new reviewers. No invented reference was found. The main finding was that results had been labelled by inconsistent standards. In response the annex now has **nine written labelling rules**, applied to every tradition alike, and every result was re-derived under them. Labels that changed: Hinduism L1 and L3, Judaism and Christianity L1 (now "contradicted", as a contradiction for one relationship is a contradiction); Stoicism L1 (now "contradicted", like Exodus 21:20 to 21); Jainism L2 and L3 (now "unclear"); Buddhism L2 and L3 (now "contradicted in one branch", Theravāda); Zoroastrianism L1 ("unclear") and L2 ("contradicted in one branch", after the Denkard Book 8 slave passage was found); Bahá'í Faith L3 ("unclear"). The Torah's captive-woman and spoil texts were added, so that Judaism and Christianity are weighed as Islam is. Further texts found or corrected are listed in the annex's Independent check section.
- **Result.** None of the three wordings can be admitted as the texts stand. *No cruelty* is the nearest (taught in seven traditions; contradicted in four; unclear in two), but this depends on reading "injure" as harm beyond moderate correction. A lower floor ("do not kill, maim, starve or torment anyone in your power") was tried, as the method requires, and still fails. A Register entry (Q-002) is proposed and waits for the project lead's choice of wording.
- **Article I, version 0.4.** Plain-language status box; the floor now says that household and workplace authority is covered and that owning people and hitting are still to be decided, and no longer presents the widest reading as the plain one; section E and decisions D1 and D2 rewritten; section G gives the reformist and community readings of Yasna 31.18 and 33.1 to 2; row 3f records the Moulton provenance; a record of the second check added.
- `HYPOTHESIS.md`, `register/q-001-golden-rule.md` and `candidates.md` updated to match.

### Project lead decisions on Article I (D1 to D3)
- **D3 (does a wider duty not to harm count?): yes.** Now a general rule in `METHOD.md` (section 3, "Wider rules"), with the condition, already applied in practice, that the wider duty must reach everyone the principle reaches.
- **D1 (Tier 2 texts accepted by one branch): left to the project's judgement.** The tier and branch rules were kept as written; the four reasons are recorded in `sources/corpus.md` (now version 1.1, no text added or moved) and summarised in `METHOD.md` (section 2). For Article I, Zoroastrianism is to be re-tested in the Avesta itself.
- **D2 (slavery and household authority): include them.** The floor is to cover slavery and authority within households, with the exact wording to be decided by the project lead after seeing what each tradition's texts say. Research started.
- Register Q-001 updated to record the decisions.
- **Zoroastrianism re-tested in the Avesta (follow-up to D1).** One researcher and one independent verifier searched the Gathas and Yasna Haptanghaiti (accepted by every branch) and the Younger Avesta. Result: no Tier 1 text teaches a duty not to harm anyone above the Implied level, and in the base translation Yasna 33.2 and 47.4 point the other way for the follower of the Lie. Article I gains row 3f (Gathas on fury and cruelty, Implied), and section G and decision D1 are rewritten; a misattribution of the reformist reading of Yasna 46.6 (to Taraporewala, when it is D. J. Irani's) is corrected.
- `sources/corpus.md`: the list of Gathas corrected to include Yasna 53 (the fifth Gatha), which had been left out by mistake. `sources/translations.md`: records the Bartholomae English on avesta.org as a philological text actually used, and how translators divide on Yasna 33.2, 46.6 and 47.4.

### Independent check of Article I and Register Q-001; revisions (step 5, method step 7)
- Five independent reviewers, each given only the finished files, checked draft Article I and Register entry Q-001: three verified every citation by groups of traditions and searched for missed counter-evidence, one reviewed wording, neutrality and logic, and one reviewed the Register entry for fairness. Their findings and how each was addressed are recorded in the article's "Independent check" section.
- A further verification pass confirmed most of the newly raised counter-evidence and the traditions' own reconciliations; items it could not confirm are marked "not yet verified" in the article.
- **Article I, version 0.2 (draft, not admitted).** Retitled "Do Not Harm Others in Ways You Would Not Accept". The requirement now covers personal conduct and leaves lawful authority, war, household authority and slavery, the beginning and end of life, and animals unsettled. A written counting rule was added and every row re-rated by it. Rows corrected, added (Yasna 43.1, Bodhicaryāvatāra 8.90, Saman Suttam 24) or marked UNVERIFIED (Shayast-ne-Shayast 13.29). Counter-evidence rewritten into thirteen themed sections, adding slavery, household discipline, apostasy and heresy, revenge, infanticide, shunning, the Báb's laws and others, each with the tradition's own reconciliation and its verification status. Three decisions for the project lead recorded (D1 to D3).
- **Register Q-001, version 0.2.** One standard applied to every tradition for the full form (eight meet; Taoism borderline; Digambara Jainism open; Sikhism and secular humanism not shown; Zoroastrianism Tier 2 only). Fairer statements of the Zoroastrian (traditionalist and reformist), Sikh, Taoist and humanist positions. Decomposition expanded to thirteen parts, including two possibly axiomatic questions (grading treatment by allegiance; one's own wishes as the measure). The procedural tier question moved out of the decomposition and framed as a general rule.
- `HYPOTHESIS.md` updated: no irreducible difference confirmed; two possibly axiomatic parts isolated; the slavery and household question (D2) flagged as the first place a real difference may lie.
- Follow-up verification of the newly raised counter-evidence: 45 of 54 items confirmed. One reviewer claim was corrected (Pahlavi Question 76 concerns those guilty of heinous sins, not apostates as such). Further sources added on household discipline (Bahá'í, Hanafi and al-Nawawī) and on Tao Te Ching 5. Seven items remain marked "not yet verified".
- `sources/corpus.md`: Humanist Manifesto II flagged as historical (the American Humanist Association treats Manifestos I and II as superseded by Manifesto III). It stays in Tier 1 as a founding text; row 13a of Article I carries the same flag.

### Article I drafted and Register entry Q-001 opened (step 5, method steps 1 to 6)
- Took C01 (the Golden Rule) through the method. Research by one researcher and one independent verifier per tradition, using web search because direct access to the reference sites was not available in this session.
- The full negative form failed for secular humanism (founding texts silent on the self-as-measure test) and, under the tier and branch rules, for Zoroastrianism (stated only in Tier 2 Pahlavi texts). A lower floor, "Do not harm anyone in a way you would not want to be harmed yourself", was tried; Zoroastrianism still failed on the tier rule, so the article was drafted and held, and Register entry Q-001 was opened.
- `candidates.md`: C01 marked as in the Register, with Article I drafted at the lower floor and held.

### Candidate list (step 4)
- Replaced the `candidates.md` placeholder with 28 candidate principles: the 15 seeds from the brief and 13 further principles that recur across many traditions, found in the first research session.
- Proposed a floor wording for every candidate.
- Split four seeds whose parts are likely to fare differently (protect the vulnerable / the stranger / the guest; anger / humility; fair dealing / just judgement; do not waste / care for the earth), giving 33 test items in all.

- Recorded unverified research leads for each candidate, clearly marked as leads, not evidence.
