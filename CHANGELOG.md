# Changelog

All changes to the Code, the Register and the method are recorded here, newest first. Dates are in ISO format (YYYY-MM-DD).

## 2026-10-13

### Independent check of Article I and Register Q-001; revisions (step 5, method step 7)
- Five independent reviewers, each given only the finished files, checked draft Article I and Register entry Q-001: three verified every citation by groups of traditions and searched for missed counter-evidence, one reviewed wording, neutrality and logic, and one reviewed the Register entry for fairness. Their findings and how each was addressed are recorded in the article's "Independent check" section.
- A further verification pass confirmed most of the newly raised counter-evidence and the traditions' own reconciliations; items it could not confirm are marked "not yet verified" in the article.
- **Article I, version 0.2 (draft, not admitted).** Retitled "Do Not Harm Others in Ways You Would Not Accept". The requirement now covers personal conduct and leaves lawful authority, war, household authority and slavery, the beginning and end of life, and animals unsettled. A written counting rule was added and every row re-rated by it. Rows corrected, added (Yasna 43.1, Bodhicaryāvatāra 8.90, Saman Suttam 24) or marked UNVERIFIED (Shayast-ne-Shayast 13.29). Counter-evidence rewritten into thirteen themed sections, adding slavery, household discipline, apostasy and heresy, revenge, infanticide, shunning, the Báb's laws and others, each with the tradition's own reconciliation and its verification status. Three decisions for the project lead recorded (D1 to D3).
- **Register Q-001, version 0.2.** One standard applied to every tradition for the full form (eight meet; Taoism borderline; Digambara Jainism open; Sikhism and secular humanism not shown; Zoroastrianism Tier 2 only). Fairer statements of the Zoroastrian (traditionalist and reformist), Sikh, Taoist and humanist positions. Decomposition expanded to thirteen parts, including two possibly axiomatic questions (grading treatment by allegiance; one's own wishes as the measure). The procedural tier question moved out of the decomposition and framed as a general rule.
- `HYPOTHESIS.md` updated: no irreducible difference confirmed; two possibly axiomatic parts isolated; the slavery and household question (D2) flagged as the first place a real difference may lie.

### Article I drafted and Register entry Q-001 opened (step 5, method steps 1 to 6)
- Took C01 (the Golden Rule) through the method. Research by one researcher and one independent verifier per tradition, using web search because direct access to the reference sites was not available in this session.
- The full negative form failed for secular humanism (founding texts silent on the self-as-measure test) and, under the tier and branch rules, for Zoroastrianism (stated only in Tier 2 Pahlavi texts). A lower floor, "Do not harm anyone in a way you would not want to be harmed yourself", was tried; Zoroastrianism still failed on the tier rule, so the article was drafted and held, and Register entry Q-001 was opened.
- `candidates.md`: C01 marked as in the Register, with Article I drafted at the lower floor and held.

### Taoism corpus and translations completed (step 2, follow-up)
- Completed the Taoism sections of `sources/corpus.md` and `sources/translations.md`, which had been marked pending.
- Tier 1 unchanged (Tao Te Ching; Zhuangzi), with the Zhuangzi Inner Chapters weighted most and early-manuscript variants recorded. Tier 2 keeps the Ganying Pian and adds the classical commentaries (Heshang Gong, Wang Bi, Guo Xiang), the Liezi (flagged) and religious Taoist precept texts.
- Translation: James Legge (1891), public domain, checked on ctext.org.

### Candidate list (step 4)
- Replaced the `candidates.md` placeholder with 28 candidate principles: the 15 seeds from the brief and 13 further principles that recur across many traditions, found in the first research session.
- Proposed a floor wording for every candidate.
- Split four seeds whose parts are likely to fare differently (protect the vulnerable / the stranger / the guest; anger / humility; fair dealing / just judgement; do not waste / care for the earth), giving 33 test items in all.
- Merged five narrower principles into the candidate they are a case of (honest weights, fair wages, returning trusts, care for the sick, patience), and recorded two that were considered and not listed (keeping confidences; seeking knowledge).
- Flagged belief risks (ego, gratitude to God, sacredness of the earth, envy) and likely contested candidates.
- Recorded unverified research leads for each candidate, clearly marked as leads, not evidence.

### Source corpus and translations (step 2)
- Replaced the placeholders in `sources/corpus.md` and `sources/translations.md`.
- Confirmed the proposed corpus for each tradition and refined it, with reasons. The five changes that alter what can count on its own are: Tattvārtha Sūtra moved to Tier 1 (Jainism); Seneca's moral essays added to Tier 1 (Stoicism); Shia sources added to Tier 2 (Islam); Gleanings moved to Tier 1 (Bahá'í Faith); A Secular Humanist Declaration (1980) moved to Tier 1 (secular humanism).
- Recorded, in advance of any article, where a Tier 2 text is treated as primary by its tradition (Talmud for Rabbinic Judaism; Pahlavi texts for traditionalist Zoroastrians; Great Learning and Doctrine of the Mean; the authentic Sunnah in Islam; ʿAbdu'l-Bahá's writings).
- The Tirukkural is recorded as context only and is not counted for Hinduism, because its religious affiliation is disputed.
- Chose a public-domain or freely accessible translation for every text, and noted where translation choice materially changes meaning.
- The Taoism sections were left marked as pending, because that research had not finished when this step was committed.

This records the method; it does not change the brief's rule that only verified citations count.
