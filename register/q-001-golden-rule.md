# Open Question 001: Should a person never treat anyone in a way they would not want to be treated themselves?

This is the Golden Rule in its full negative form: not only "do not harm others in ways you would not want to be harmed" (the draft [Article I](../code/articles/article-01-golden-rule.md)), but "do not treat anyone in *any* way you would not want to be treated", which also covers courtesy, honour, company and other treatment that is unwanted without being harmful.

*So far as it has been studied, the open issue is mostly about evidence and about how texts are counted, not about traditions teaching the opposite. But several parts below have not yet been examined fully, and two may turn out to involve real differences of value.*

**Why this is in the Register.** The full form says more than "do not harm". A duty not to harm, however strict, does not by itself give this rule; only a teaching that uses oneself as the measure (or that puts oneself in the other person's place) does. Judged by that one standard, applied to every tradition alike, eight traditions clearly teach the full form. Five do not yet meet it, for different reasons: Zoroastrianism (stated only in Tier 2 texts), Taoism (borderline), Jainism (open for one branch), Sikhism and secular humanism (they teach a wide duty not to harm and to respect everyone, but the measure itself is not shown in their primary texts).

**Fair statements of the positions not yet meeting the full form**

*Zoroastrianism, traditionalist (Parsi and Iranian).* The ethical teaching of our faith has always been carried by the Pahlavi books, which preserve the priests' interpretation (*zand*) of the Avesta. "Do not do to others what is not good for oneself" is the very definition of a good nature (Denkard 6; Dadestan-i Denig 94.5), and the Shayast-ne-Shayast (13.29) is reported to read it into the Gathas themselves, as the substance of one of Zarathushtra's hymns (this reference is not yet verified; Article I, row 3c). The Gathas teach it as the *zand* explains them.

*Zoroastrianism, reformist ("Gathas-only").* The Gathas, Zarathushtra's own words, are our only binding scripture, and they teach this rule: "Happiness to him who brings happiness to others" (Yasna 43.1, as rendered in reformist and Iranian priestly teaching). Their call to oppose the followers of the Lie is a call to resist wrongdoing, not to mistreat people.

*Sikhism.* The Guru teaches that the One dwells in every heart, so we must speak no harsh word, break no heart, frighten no one, bear no ill will, and pray daily for the good of all (*sarbat da bhala*). That asks more than a formula about our own wishes.

*Taoism.* The sage has no fixed mind of his own and makes the people's heart his heart; he is good to the good and to the not-good alike. Measuring others by one's own likes is exactly what Zhuangzi warns against: the marquis who fed a seabird as he would feed himself killed it.

*Secular humanism.* Humanist ethics rests on reason, empathy and compassion, not fixed rules. Our declarations commit us to the equal worth and dignity of every person, to freedom limited by the equal rights of others, and to "helping and not harming". Many humanists live by the Golden Rule and humanist education presents it, while warning that other people's wishes, not only our own, must guide how we treat them.

**A procedural question for the project lead (outside the four types)**

When one major branch of a tradition accepts a Tier 2 text that states a principle, and another branch's primary texts do not state it, does the tradition "clearly teach" it? Under the rules as written in `sources/corpus.md`, no. (Since 2026-10-13 the Pahlavi books are in any case not treated as primary even by the traditionalist branch; see Part 2.) This already affects Zoroastrianism (Pahlavi books) and the Digambara branch of Jainism (Saman Suttam). `sources/corpus.md` also says that no corpus rule may be changed to rescue or sink a particular article, so any decision must be made as a general rule, on its merits, for all thirteen traditions and all later articles.

*Decided 2026-10-13.* The project lead left this to the project's judgement. The tier and branch rules were kept as written, for the reasons recorded in [`sources/corpus.md`](../sources/corpus.md) ("Review of the tier and branch rules"). For this question that means: Zoroastrianism meets the fuller rule only if a text that both traditionalists and reformists accept teaches it, and Digambara Jainism only if a Digambara or shared text does. Parts 1, 2 and 7 therefore stay open as textual questions.

**Axiomatic residue**

None identified so far, but the search for it has not been systematic. Parts 8 and 9 are candidates. They would prove reducible if each tradition's own authorities confine the graded-treatment texts to particular circumstances (Part 8), or if a measure combining one's own wishes with attention to the other person's is found in every tradition (Part 9). They would prove axiomatic if a tradition holds, as a foundational value, that people of another faith or allegiance may rightly be treated with less courtesy or honour.

**Candidate floor (if any)**

"Do not harm anyone in a way you would not want to be harmed yourself." This is drafted as [Article I](../code/articles/article-01-golden-rule.md). The project lead's decisions of 2026-10-13 are recorded there: the tier and branch rules stand (so Zoroastrianism must meet the floor in texts every branch accepts), a wider duty not to harm counts, and the floor now includes authority within households. On the same day the project lead left the remaining decisions to the project's judgement: the household wording is *no cruelty* (do not injure, torment, starve, overwork or humiliate anyone in your power), and owning people, physical punishment and sexual relations without consent within relationships of power went to the Register as [Q-002](q-002-households-and-slavery.md). Because Zoroastrianism (decision D1) and four traditions' texts on households (Q-002) do not meet the floor, the article's verdict is Register: it stays a draft and is not admitted.
